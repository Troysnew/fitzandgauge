# Sources: How do cats land on their feet?

- https://doi.org/10.1002/ar.70165
  Higurashi et al., The Anatomical Record (2026): in cat spines the chest (thoracic) part twists easily and the lower back (lumbar) is stiffer. Filming of live cats showed the front trunk finishing its rotation before the back trunk, and the authors suggest the spine suits this.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260501073243/https://anatomypubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ar.70165
- https://ds0n.cc.yamaguchi-u.ac.jp/~celldesign/en_news/260430en/
  Yamaguchi University English press release (2026-04-30): the university's own summary of the study, finding the thoracic vertebrae have a very high capacity for twisting.
- https://www.sciencefocus.com/science/the-enduring-puzzle-of-why-cats-always-land-on-their-feet
  BBC Science Focus, by physicist Gregory J. Gbur: Marey's 1894 photographs showed a cat starting with no spin and still turning over. In the bend-and-twist model the front and rear halves twist in opposite directions and their twists largely cancel.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260413110643/https://www.sciencefocus.com/science/the-enduring-puzzle-of-why-cats-always-land-on-their-feet
- https://www.discovermagazine.com/why-do-cats-always-land-on-their-feet-44885
  Discover Magazine (2023), quoting physicist Greg Gbur: the cat rotates its front and back halves in opposite directions so the spins cancel. Early rigid-body analysis failed because cats are supple.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260228005544/https://www.discovermagazine.com/why-do-cats-always-land-on-their-feet-44885
- https://science.charlotte.edu/2026/03/09/greg-gbur-discusses-why-cats-always-land-on-their-feet/
  UNC Charlotte College of Science news (2026-03-09): front and back halves turn in opposite directions. The 2026 study footage showed the front half finishing its rotation before the rear, which Gbur says gives the tuck-and-turn model a little more credence.
- https://nautil.us/why-cats-always-land-on-their-feet-1278922
  Nautilus (2026): the front of the body rotates first and the rear follows. The thoracic spine is very flexible while the lumbar spine is relatively rigid.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260513073453/https://nautil.us/why-cats-always-land-on-their-feet-1278922
