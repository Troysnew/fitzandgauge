# Sources: Case Files: The Skyscraper With a Secret (Citicorp Center, 1978)

- https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1995/05/29/the-fifty-nine-story-crisis
  Joe Morgenstern, The New Yorker (May 29, 1995): the main account of the building, the damper, the bolted joints, the repair and the secrecy. Most crisis figures (40%, 160%, 1 in 16, 1 in 55, the 700-year storm) are LeMessurier's own account.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20230301030235/https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1995/05/29/the-fifty-nine-story-crisis
- http://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/2582.pdf
  NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission designation report (2016): the building's height, columns, braces, light steel frame and damper, the welded repair, and the 2016 landmark designation.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20251126113033/https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/2582.pdf
- https://onlineethics.org/cases/engineers-and-scientists-behaving-well/william-lemessurier-fifty-nine-story-crisis-lesson
  Online Ethics Center (National Academy of Engineering), Whitbeck and Plosky: the case study of the crisis, the repair and the student phone call, including Lee DeCarolis's own account. Its figures derive from the New Yorker article.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250215063419/https://onlineethics.org/cases/engineers-and-scientists-behaving-well/william-lemessurier-fifty-nine-story-crisis-lesson
- https://theaiatrust.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/LeMessurier-Stands-Tall-A-Case-Study-in-Professional-Ethics.pdf
  AIA Trust, Vardaro, "LeMessurier Stands Tall": supports the bolt change, the repair and the quiet handling of the crisis, and notes that his partners said diagonal winds were considered in design.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250919022014/https://theaiatrust.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/LeMessurier-Stands-Tall-A-Case-Study-in-Professional-Ethics.pdf
- http://www.crosscurrents.org/kremer2002.htm
  Eugene Kremer, Cross Currents (2002): an independent re-examination of the Citicorp case, supporting the damper, the code and the disputed question of who approved the bolt change.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260219150516/http://crosscurrents.org/kremer2002.htm
- https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24773/chapter/34
  National Academy of Engineering, Memorial Tributes vol. 21 (2017), William J. LeMessurier: supports the building's structure, the wind-load check and the bolt problem (it gives about 920 feet for the height, where other sources say about 915).
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250508162953/https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/24773/chapter/34
- https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/structural-integrity/
  99% Invisible, "Structural Integrity" (2014), episode page: interviews with Diane Hartley, David Billington and Joel Weinstein supporting the student thesis, and the 1-in-55 figure.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260930034910/https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/structural-integrity/
- https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/structural-integrity-2/transcript/
  99% Invisible, "Structural Integrity" (2014), transcript: the same interviews, including Hartley raising her thesis with an engineer at LeMessurier's office and the evacuation planning.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260612112352/https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/structural-integrity-2/transcript/
- https://ktvz.com/entertainment/cnn-style/2025/08/15/this-new-york-skyscraper-had-a-1-in-16-chance-of-collapse-only-one-man-knew/
  Oscar Holland, CNN Style (Aug 15, 2025), read in a syndicated copy: supports Hartley's and DeCarolis's roles and that neither student found the bolt problem.
- https://www.enr.com/articles/60648-author-details-citicorp-tower-design-error-and-the-race-to-fix-it
  Engineering News-Record (2025) on Greenburg's The Great Miscalculation: supports the 1-in-16 figure, the night welding, the student call, and the dispute over who approved the bolt change.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250723205010/https://www.enr.com/articles/60648-author-details-citicorp-tower-design-error-and-the-race-to-fix-it
- https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/17631-the-great-miscalculation-offers-a-riveting-account-of-a-would-be-crisis-in-midtown-manhattan
  Architectural Record (July 2025), review of The Great Miscalculation: supports the 1-in-16 figure, the night repairs, the student accounts and the building's present status.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20251006112948/https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/17631-the-great-miscalculation-offers-a-riveting-account-of-a-would-be-crisis-in-midtown-manhattan
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/wind-effects-tall-building-square-cross-section-and-mid-side-base-columns-database
  NIST researchers (Park, Duthinh, Simiu and Yeo, ASCE Journal of Structural Engineering, 2019), abstract page: modern analysis of a similar building found face-on winds, not corner winds, were the bigger load.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260615183105/https://www.nist.gov/publications/wind-effects-tall-building-square-cross-section-and-mid-side-base-columns-database
- https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/blown-away-revisiting-famous-engineering-case
  Duthinh, NIST Taking Measure blog (2019): the modern reassessment of the wind loads, Hartley's story, and that the full story came out in 1995.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260812015548/https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/blown-away-revisiting-famous-engineering-case
- https://www.engineersireland.ie/Engineers-Journal/Civil/modern-reassessment-of-the-citicorp-building-design-wind-loads
  Duthinh, Engineers Journal (2020), public part: calls diagonal winds "the major (but not the only)" reason for the repair, and describes the braces.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260208225848/https://www.engineersireland.ie/Engineers-Journal/Civil/modern-reassessment-of-the-citicorp-building-design-wind-loads
- https://www.engineersireland.ie/Engineers-Journal/Civil/citicorp-center-tower-how-failure-was-averted
  Sean Brady, Engineers Journal: supports the welded plates, the night welding hours, the plywood enclosures, the emergency power, Hurricane Ella, the October 1978 finish and the $2 million settlement.
- https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/article/2007/07/william-j-lemessurier
  ASCE Civil Engineering magazine (July 2007), on LeMessurier: supports the welded repair, the damper kept running, the 700-year storm estimate and the case's use in ethics teaching.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260715094016/https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/article/2007/07/william-j-lemessurier
- https://betterworld.mit.edu/spectrum/issues/winter-1999/the-right-stuff/
  MIT Spectrum, "The Right Stuff" (Winter 1999): supports the quiet evacuation planning and the $2 million settlement.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261009084846/http://betterworld.mit.edu/spectrum/issues/winter-1999/the-right-stuff/
- https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/buildingbig/wonder/structure/citicorp.html
  PBS Building Big, Citicorp Center: supports the welded steel plates and Hurricane Ella.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260827153618/http://www.pbs.org/wgbh/buildingbig/wonder/structure/citicorp.html
- https://thecrimson.com/article/1978/11/8/newsstands-sell-times-rapidly-as-readers
  Harvard Crimson (Nov 8, 1978): supports the 1978 New York newspaper strike.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20251109131835/https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1978/11/8/newsstands-sell-times-rapidly-as-readers/
- https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/hurdat/hurdat2-1851-2025-02272026.txt
  NOAA National Hurricane Center HURDAT2 best-track data: Hurricane Ella's track in 1978, which stayed several hundred miles from New York.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261003053307/https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/hurdat/hurdat2-1851-2025-02272026.txt
