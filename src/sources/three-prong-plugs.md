# Sources: Why do some plugs have a third round prong?

- https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/electric/bregnd.html
  HyperPhysics, Georgia State University: the third wire is the ground, connected to the appliance's metal case and not part of the normal current path. If the hot wire shorts to the case, high current flows and trips the breaker; without it the breaker may not trip.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260930173812/https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/electric/bregnd.html
- https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-8-electrical-safety-systems-and-devices
  OpenStax College Physics 2e, section 23.8: the ground wire keeps the case near zero volts if worn insulation lets the hot wire touch it. With the ground connection intact the circuit breaker will trip; without it a shock can occur.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260608050632/https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-8-electrical-safety-systems-and-devices
- https://www.osha.gov/etools/construction/electrical-incidents/grounding
  U.S. OSHA Construction eTool: grounding means intentionally creating a low-resistance path to the earth. If a tool's metal frame becomes energized, the equipment ground provides another path for the current.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260415094717/https://www.osha.gov/etools/construction/electrical-incidents/grounding
