# Sources: Why Is Hoover Dam Full of Pipes?

- https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/faqs/damfaqs.html
  Bureau of Reclamation, the dam's owner, Hoover Dam FAQ, "The Dam": "more than 582 miles" of one-inch steel pipe in the concrete, the hard, durable canyon rock, about 21,000 workers, the curve, water pressure of about 45,000 pounds per square foot at the bottom, "both gravity action and horizontal arch action", a weight of "more than 6,600,000 tons", the heat of setting concrete, "an estimated 215 blocks" forming a one-piece structure, and cooling finished in March 1935. Most of the dam's own numbers in this episode come from Reclamation, so they count as one voice, not several.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261025062144/https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/faqs/damfaqs.html
- https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/81000382_text
  National Historic Landmark nomination for Hoover Dam (National Park Service), prepared by a Reclamation archeologist in 1979 and revised by the Park Service in 1985: the pipe, the river's floods and the 1905 flood into the Salton basin, the dam's jobs of storing water and making power, its 660-foot base and its curve, the "ice-cold water" in the pipes, and the warning that the concrete would have taken "more than a century" to cool and "would have shrunk and cracked as it cooled". It contains some errors, such as "four tunnels on each side", which are not used.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260830105806/https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/81000382_text
- https://www.nrmca.org/america250/hoover-dam/
  National Ready Mixed Concrete Association, an industry group, "Historic Piece: Hoover Dam": the pipe, the columns, the ice-cold cooling water, and concrete cores taken in 1995 that "continued to slowly gain strength". It largely restates Reclamation's figures.
- https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/history/essays/concrete.html
  Bureau of Reclamation history essay, "Concrete": the "1-inch thin-walled" pipe, the five-foot lifts, river water and then ice-cold water from a refrigeration plant on the lower cofferdam, the calculation that a "single continuous pour" would take "125 years for the concrete to cool to ambient temperatures" and would "crack and crumble away", and the pipes being "cut off and pressure grouted" as each block cooled.
