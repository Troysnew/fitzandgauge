# Sources: How do woodpeckers not hurt their brains?

- https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/07/220714145037.htm
  Cell Press release for Van Wassenbergh et al., Current Biology, 2022: woodpecker heads work like stiff hammers and do not absorb the shock; the small brain keeps loads below concussion levels.
- https://phys.org/news/2022-07-woodpeckers-stiff-safety-helmets.html
  Cross-check of the same findings.
- https://www.audubon.org/magazine/new-study-shakes-long-held-belief-woodpecker-hammering
  Beak and head stopped at the same rate in high-speed video; size is "the most compelling possibility".
- https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/woodpecker-skulls-dont-absorb-shock-like-previously-thought-180980426/
  Woodpecker brains are about 700 times smaller than human brains; the hammer analogy; smaller animals withstand higher decelerations.
