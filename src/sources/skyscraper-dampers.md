# Sources: Why do some skyscrapers have a giant ball inside?

- https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/774616-largest-tuned-mass-damper
  Guinness World Records on Taipei 101's damper: a pendulum weight linked by shock absorbers that swings out of phase with the building, with the dampers converting the motion into heat. It is meant to keep occupants comfortable as much as possible and to help in rare extreme events.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250701155418/https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/774616-largest-tuned-mass-damper
- https://www.thorntontomasetti.com/project/taipei-101
  Thornton Tomasetti, structural engineer of record: the tuned mass damper reduces building movement from strong winds and earthquakes and increases occupant comfort.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260606133821/https://www.thorntontomasetti.com/project/taipei-101
- https://taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2004/04/30/2003138582
  Taipei Times (2004), an independent cross-check on comfort: quotes Russ Thomas saying people will feel more comfortable working inside with the damper.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240618095735/https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2004/04/30/2003138582
- https://www.motioneering.ca/post/innovative-damping-systems-for-tall-buildings
  Motioneering, the company that designed and built Taipei 101's damper, on damping systems for tall buildings.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260510073346/https://www.motioneering.ca/post/innovative-damping-systems-for-tall-buildings
- https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna44292269
  NBC News / Live Science wire (2011), quoting Columbia University civil engineering professor Christian Meyer: no matter how strong a building is made it vibrates at some load, no modern skyscraper has been brought down by wind alone, and too much vibration makes people unwell.
- https://rwdi.com/insight/keeping-skyscrapers-from-blowing-in-the-wind/
  RWDI, wind engineers: tuned mass dampers keep slender skyscrapers comfortable for occupants by reducing movement in high winds.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260615111744/https://rwdi.com/insight/keeping-skyscrapers-from-blowing-in-the-wind/
