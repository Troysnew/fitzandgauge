# Sources: How do bees build hexagons?

- https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9906042
  Thomas C. Hales, "The Honeycomb Conjecture": among all ways to divide a flat plane into regions of equal area, the regular hexagonal grid has the least total perimeter. The proof also covers non-convex cells.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261011000701/https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9906042
- https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00900/full
  Gallo and Chittka (Queen Mary University of London): round cells such as bumblebees' waste space between cells, and honeycomb is optimal or very close to optimal for saving material. Describes two views on how the hexagon forms, and says whether bees use planning-like skills is unproven.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240122073644/https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00900/full
- https://chittkalab.sbcs.qmul.ac.uk/2023/s00359-023-01632-y.pdf
  Gallo, Bridges, Woodgate and Chittka (2023): shared hexagonal walls give maximum storage with minimal wax, cells begin as circular and become hexagonal, and the building rules remain poorly understood.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240531133148/https://chittkalab.sbcs.qmul.ac.uk/2023/s00359-023-01632-y.pdf
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00359-023-01632-y
  Publisher page for the Gallo et al. (2023) paper above, in the Journal of Comparative Physiology A.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260512102439/https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00359-023-01632-y
- https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23864500
  Karihaloo, Zhang and Wang (2013), read through its Europe PMC record: cells are circular at birth and become rounded hexagons, which the authors attribute to molten, visco-elastic wax flowing near the junctions, heated by worker bees.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240502170512/https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23864500
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00114-012-0992-3
  Bauer and Bienefeld (2013): infrared and thermographic video showed bees directly constructing hexagons with antennae, mandibles and legs, and the wax stayed below the temperature the liquid-flow model needs.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250707015645/https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00114-012-0992-3
- https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28341
  Nazzi (2016), Scientific Reports: the hexagonal shape arises from the bees' construction behavior, and the paper proposes a behavioral rule, a third view on how the shape forms.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260808180111/https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28341
- https://phys.org/news/2013-07-secrets-bee-honeycombs-revealed.html
  Phys.org coverage of the Cardiff University study, a cross-check: cells start as circles, and wax softened by worker bees flows and is pulled into hexagons at the three-wall junctions.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240623194400/https://phys.org/news/2013-07-secrets-bee-honeycombs-revealed.html
- https://discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/scientists-explain-the-amazing-process-by-which-bees-make-hexagonal-honeycombs
  Discover magazine (2014), a summary of the Karihaloo paper that adds nothing new and serves only as a second cross-check.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250718103825/https://www.discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/scientists-explain-the-amazing-process-by-which-bees-make-hexagonal-honeycombs
- https://wgbh.org/news/2013-05-16/what-is-it-about-bees-and-hexagons
  WGBH (2013): circles leave gaps, only triangles, squares and hexagons tile a flat surface with equal sides, the hexagon is the most compact, and Hales proved the conjecture in 1999.
- https://www.sciencefriday.com/educational-resources/why-do-bees-build-hexagonal-honeycomb-cells/
  Science Friday educational resource: circles leave gaps, triangles, squares and hexagons share walls with no gaps, and Hales proved the conjecture in 1999.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260312020950/https://www.sciencefriday.com/educational-resources/why-do-bees-build-hexagonal-honeycomb-cells/
