# Sources: Why do sidewalks have lines?

- https://www.pacaweb.org/uploads/documents/CrackingPIS.pdf
  Pennsylvania Aggregates and Concrete Association consumer brochure: concrete cracks most commonly from drying shrinkage, and contraction joints control where it will crack. Joint depth is about 1/4 of the slab thickness, creating a plane of weakness where the crack should form.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250709025511/https://www.pacaweb.org/uploads/documents/CrackingPIS.pdf
- https://pacaweb.org/uploads/documents/NRMCA-jointing-concrete2.pdf
  National Ready Mixed Concrete Association flyer: concrete cracks from shrinkage as it dries, and contraction joints activate cracking below the sawcut. Cut depth should be a minimum of a quarter of the slab thickness.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20251112194836/https://www.pacaweb.org/uploads/documents/NRMCA-jointing-concrete2.pdf
- https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/pavement/concrete/pubs/07031/07031.pdf
  U.S. Federal Highway Administration TechBrief (2007) on road pavement, same principle: joints prevent uncontrolled, random cracking. Sawing typically one-quarter to one-third of the slab thickness creates a weakened plane so cracks form there in a controlled manner.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20251003034831/https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/pavement/concrete/pubs/07031/07031.pdf
