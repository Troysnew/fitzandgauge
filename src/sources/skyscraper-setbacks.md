# Sources: Why do old New York skyscrapers look like wedding cakes?

- https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/about/publications/zoning-handbook/zoning-handbook-01.pdf
  NYC Department of City Planning, Zoning Handbook: the Equitable Building, erected in 1915, rose without setbacks to its full height and cast a seven-acre shadow over neighboring buildings, prompting worry about more buildings like it. New York enacted the nation's first comprehensive zoning ordinance in 1916, which fostered tiered "wedding cake" buildings.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260618114045/https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/about/publications/zoning-handbook/zoning-handbook-01.pdf
- https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/about/city-planning-history/1916_zoning_resolution.pdf
  City of New York, Building Zone Resolution (adopted July 25, 1916), the original law text: where a building covers no more than 25 percent of the lot above a given level, the part above that level is exempt from the height and setback limits.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260121121408/https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/about/city-planning-history/1916_zoning_resolution.pdf
- https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1935.pdf
  NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission, Equitable Building designation report (1996): the building rises approximately 38 stories straight up from the lot line with no setbacks, contemporaries complained it cut off sunlight from its neighbors, and it became the prime example cited in the passage of New York's zoning law, the first in the country.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260302162253/http://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/1935.pdf
- https://skyscraper.org/skyline/impact-of-1916-zoning/
  The Skyscraper Museum, Impact of 1916 Zoning: the law aimed to preserve a measure of light and air on the streets and sidewalks. Above a maximum height, buildings had to step back within a set diagonal, and a tower of unlimited height could rise over 25 percent of the lot, giving the stepped-pyramid shape.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260914025521/https://www.skyscraper.org/skyline/impact-of-1916-zoning/
- https://old.skyscraper.org/EXHIBITIONS/DOWNTOWN_NEW_YORK/CONTENT/dny_law2.htm
  The Skyscraper Museum, Downtown New York exhibition: the law was designed to protect some light and air for Manhattan's streets. Above a set height, a building had to step back within an "angle of light" plane drawn from the center of the street, and a tower of unlimited height was allowed over one-quarter of the site.
- https://thegreatestgrid.mcny.org/greatest-grid/manhattan-monumentality/294
  Museum of the City of New York, The Greatest Grid: the 1916 law required buildings to step back as they rose to protect sunlight on the street and lower stories, and allowed a tower of unlimited height on 25 percent of the lot.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250407010650/https://thegreatestgrid.mcny.org/greatest-grid/manhattan-monumentality/294
