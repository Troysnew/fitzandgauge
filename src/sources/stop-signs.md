# Sources: Why are stop signs octagons?

- https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20161031205947/http:/mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/kno-history.htm
  FHWA history: in the early 1920s, shapes were classified by danger; round for railroad crossings, octagon for STOP; drivers would respond to the shape even when they couldn't see the letters.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20170113080321/http://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/kno-history.htm
- https://magazine.northeast.aaa.com/daily/life/cars-trucks/auto-history/stop-sign-history/
  Texas A&M's Gene Hawkins: the shape plan was adopted in 1923; drivers could act on the shape without reading the words.
- https://mutcd-dr.fhwa.dot.gov/resources/interpretations/6_09_013.htm
  FHWA: the octagonal shape is reserved for the STOP message.
- https://www.allstate.com/resources/car-insurance/stop-signs
  The octagon is recognizable from the back.
- https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/ubiquitous-icons-highways-101/transcript/
  Second source: an octagon seen from behind is recognizable as a stop sign.
