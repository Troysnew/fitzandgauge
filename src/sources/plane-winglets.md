# Sources: Why do airplane wings curve up at the tips?

- https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/10666
  NASA Spinoff 2010: high pressure under the wing makes air flow up over the wingtip and spin off in a vortex, which produces induced drag. Whitcomb proposed the winglet to weaken wingtip vortices and reduce induced drag, and winglets typically give 4- to 6-percent fuel savings.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260930111137/https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/10666
- https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/armstrong/on-the-winglets-of-innovation-with-nasa-armstrong-2
  NASA (2023): the wingtip vortex is a whirlpool of air that spirals behind an airplane, causing drag. Winglets decrease it, so fuel consumption goes down and range is extended.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260328092656/https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/armstrong/on-the-winglets-of-innovation-with-nasa-armstrong-2/
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/induced-drag-coefficient/
  NASA Glenn Beginner's Guide to Aeronautics: near the wing tips, air moves from high pressure to low pressure, making tip vortices and induced drag. On modern airliners the wing tips are often bent up to form winglets to reduce it.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261015132233/https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/induced-drag-coefficient/
- https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2000-02-18-New-Winglets-Boost-Boeing-Next-Generation-737-Performance
  Boeing press release (2000): the blended winglet curves out and up from the wing tip, reducing aerodynamic drag. A 737-800 with it will burn 3 percent to 5 percent less fuel.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260611232215/https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2000-02-18-New-Winglets-Boost-Boeing-Next-Generation-737-Performance
- https://ainonline.com/aviation-news/air-transport/2009-11-15/a320-sharklets-take-nice-bite-out-fuel-burn
  AIN (2009), reporting the Airbus launch: Sharklets improve fuel burn on the A320 family by 3.5 percent on longer sectors.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250622040803/https://www.ainonline.com/aviation-news/air-transport/2009-11-15/a320-sharklets-take-nice-bite-out-fuel-burn
