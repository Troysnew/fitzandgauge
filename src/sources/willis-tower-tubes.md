# Sources: Why is the Willis Tower built from nine tubes?

- https://www.som.com/projects/willis-tower/
  Skidmore, Owings & Merrill (SOM), the tower's architect and structural engineer: the base is nine 75-foot-by-75-foot square tubes, each rigid within itself without internal supports. They are bundled together as a closed square up to the first 50 stories, then end at varying heights to create the tower's stepped form.
- https://www.architecture.org/learn/resources/buildings-of-chicago/building/willis-tower/
  Chicago Architecture Center: Fazlur Khan and Bruce Graham used the bundled tube, a cluster of nine tubes connected together to act as a single unit. The tubes support one another, strengthening the structure as a whole.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240520052518/https://www.architecture.org/learn/resources/buildings-of-chicago/building/willis-tower/
- https://www.lehigh.edu/~infrk/2011.08.article.html
  Yasmin Sabina Khan, Fazlur Khan's daughter and biographer, Lehigh University (2011): Khan's framed tube is a perimeter shell of closely spaced columns and stiff beams. His bundled tube concept began with the Sears Tower (now the Willis Tower) and gave remarkable structural stiffness.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261009042116/https://www.lehigh.edu/~infrk/2011.08.article.html
- https://engineerguy.com/comm/4585.htm
  William S. Hammack, engineering professor, radio commentary on the Sears Tower (2004): as a building gets taller it becomes more flexible in the wind. Khan's idea was to build it from square tubes, each acting as a building in itself, and bundling nine together gives strong resistance to bending.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250520005048/https://engineerguy.com/comm/4585.htm
- https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,907406,00.html
  Time magazine (1973): nine square tubes joined together form the basic structure of the Sears Tower, and Khan demonstrated the idea by grasping a bundle of nine upright cigarettes. The architects ended some of the tubes at different floor levels partway up.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250216220140/https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,907406,00.html
- https://chipublib.org/technology-that-changed-chicago-fazlur-khan-and-tubular-designs-for-skyscrapers
  Chicago Public Library blog (2014): Khan's tubular designs work like an exoskeleton, a tough structure wrapped around the building that resists the tilting and wind loads on super tall buildings. The design is called bundled tubes.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240727030311/https://www.chipublib.org/technology-that-changed-chicago-fazlur-khan-and-tubular-designs-for-skyscrapers/
- https://www.skyscrapercenter.com/building/willis-tower/169
  Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat, The Skyscraper Center: the Willis Tower, formerly the Sears Tower, is an all-steel building completed in 1974 and designed by SOM. Its client's space needs resulted in a bundled tube structure, the first of its kind.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261011050209/https://www.skyscrapercenter.com/building/willis-tower/169
