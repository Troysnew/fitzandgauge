# Sources: Why do big ships have a round bump under the front?

- https://www.boatdesign.net/attachments/ship-design-i-2-bulbous-bow-design-ventura-2010-pdf.162890/
  Manuel Ventura, IST Lisbon lecture slides: the wave system generated by the bulb interferes with the ship's own wave system and reduces the bow wave. The bulb shape must be adjusted to the design conditions, and at low speeds the effect of the bulb is generally negative.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260517214724/https://www.boatdesign.net/attachments/ship-design-i-2-bulbous-bow-design-ventura-2010-pdf.162890/
- https://wartsila.com/encyclopedia/term/bow
  Wartsila Encyclopedia, a manufacturer: a bulb is designed to reduce wave making resistance by developing an independent wave system that reduces the total wave system. It has a negative effect on power consumption when only partly submerged or not designed properly.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260616192040/https://www.wartsila.com/encyclopedia/term/bow
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-navigation/article/first-escort-tractor-voith-tug-with-a-bulbous-bow-analysis-and-consequences/3C7B7FEFEFC9050CAA6A2D6FFFBF86B2
  Iglesias Baniela and Platero Diaz, The Journal of Navigation (2008), peer-reviewed, abstract only: each bulb is generally tuned to the expected operating speed of the ship.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20180614235510/https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-navigation/article/first-escort-tractor-voith-tug-with-a-bulbous-bow-analysis-and-consequences/3C7B7FEFEFC9050CAA6A2D6FFFBF86B2
- https://www.marineinsight.com/naval-architecture/why-do-ships-have-bulbous-bow/
  Marine Insight, a trade explainer: the bulb creates its own wave, and designers aim for destructive interference with the bow wave. At very low Froude numbers bulbous bows have been found to increase drag, and bulbs are not efficient at all service speeds.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260214135142/https://www.marineinsight.com/naval-architecture/why-do-ships-have-bulbous-bow/
- https://forcetechnology.com/insights/cases/retrofitting-a-new-bulbous-bow
  FORCE Technology, a Danish hydrodynamics test institute: ships have traditionally been optimised for one single design point, and ships with bulbous bows are sensitive to off-design operation. Maersk Line exchanged the bow on five 4,500 TEU vessels and five 8,500 vessels.
- https://shipsmonthly.com/news/off-with-the-bulbous-bows
  Ships Monthly (2013), quoting Maersk Maritime Technology's Steffen Hartvig Nielsen: large container ships are typically designed for speeds of 25 knots, but with slow steaming the bulbous bows are often out of shape and generate high resistance.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250426182951/https://shipsmonthly.com/news/off-with-the-bulbous-bows/
- https://shipandbunker.com/news/world/305970-maersk-line-adjusts-bows-for-slow-steaming
  Ship & Bunker (2013): Maersk Line says bulbous bows must be designed for a particular speed range, and it was modifying the bows of five Boston-class ships designed for high speeds to suit slow steaming.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240724085646/https://shipandbunker.com/news/world/305970-maersk-line-adjusts-bows-for-slow-steaming
- https://en.portnews.ru/news/152307
  PortNews (Dec 2012), citing SinoShipNews: Maersk Line was upgrading the bulbous bows of 10 container ships at Qingdao Beihai to cut fuel use while slow steaming.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20121230073519/http://en.portnews.ru:80/news/152307/
- https://officerofthewatch.com/2013/03/12/maersk-ships-get-new-bulbous-bow/
  Officer of the Watch blog (2013), a weak corroborating source: the whole bulbous bow of Maersk Brownsville was replaced with one matched to the ship's actual speed profile.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20230529032639/https://officerofthewatch.com/2013/03/12/maersk-ships-get-new-bulbous-bow/
