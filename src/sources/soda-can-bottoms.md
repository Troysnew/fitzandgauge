# Sources: Why is the bottom of a soda can curved inward?

- https://patents.google.com/patent/US4294373A/en
  US Patent 4,294,373, Ball Corporation (granted 1981): the domed bottom gives stability against the internal pressure of the contents, since a thin drawn metal can tends to buckle outward under carbonated pressure. A flat bottom is not economically feasible, because the metal would need extra thickness to keep from deforming.
- https://csmonitor.com/Science/Science-Notebook/2015/0414/The-surprising-science-behind-the-aluminum-soda-can
  The Christian Science Monitor (2015), reporting the explanation of Bill Hammack, professor of chemical engineering at the University of Illinois: the bottom is pressed into a concave dome, which lets the can withstand greater pressures than if it were flat, and the internal pressure lets the can be strong in spite of its thin walls.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260304050238/https://www.csmonitor.com/Science/Science-Notebook/2015/0414/The-surprising-science-behind-the-aluminum-soda-can
- https://patents.google.com/patent/US4426013A/en
  US Patent 4,426,013, Jos. Schlitz Brewing Co. (granted 1984): under internal pressure, a can's bottom wall will stretch and bulge outward. Makers formed an upward dome because it resists deformation under internal pressure.
- https://patents.google.com/patent/US7398894B2/en
  US Patent 7,398,894, Metal Container Corporation (granted 2008): the domed bottom has been found to resist deformation under internal pressure, and the bottom design must allow cans to be stacked in more than one layer.
- https://patents.google.com/patent/US4685582A/en
  US Patent 4,685,582, National Can Corporation (granted 1987): the bottom profile is designed so the bottom of one can nests within the end of the can below, which prevents wobbling when filled cans are stacked.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20210920153642/https://patents.google.com/patent/US4685582A/en
- https://patents.google.com/patent/US5477977
  US Patent 5,477,977, Reynolds Metals Company (granted 1995): lists as a can-bottom design requirement the ability to nest against the seamed end of the can below when stacked, alongside standing stably on a flat surface.
- https://packagingschool.com/lessons/the-beverage-can-forming-process
  Packaging School lesson (2025): to hold highly pressurized contents, an arc is formed into the bottom of the can, the dome, which spreads the pressure across that arc.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260618071612/https://packagingschool.com/lessons/the-beverage-can-forming-process
- https://aluminium-guide.com/aluminium-beverage-cans/
  Aluminium Guide: the bottom of the can takes a dome shape to resist the internal pressure, and the base is profiled so it stacks on the matching lid.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240610195459/https://aluminium-guide.com/aluminium-beverage-cans/
