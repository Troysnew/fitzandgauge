# Sources: Why do most wind turbines have three blades?

- https://acee.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/AndlingerDistillate_Article5.pdf
  Princeton University Andlinger Center: the choice of three blades is a trade-off between efficiency and cost. More blades sweep more air but add weight, complexity and cost, while two blades would need to sweep through the air more quickly, causing noise and vibration problems.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20231016120646/https://acee.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/AndlingerDistillate_Article5.pdf
- http://www.windpower.org/en/tour/design/concepts.htm
  Danish Wind Industry Association guided tour (2003): engineers avoid even numbers of blades mainly for stability, since an odd-bladed rotor acts like a disc when calculating dynamic properties. Two-bladed designs save cost and weight but require higher rotational speed for the same energy output.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20090827122205/http://www.windpower.org:80/EN/tour/design/concepts.htm
- https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1227612
  University of Toledo final technical report to the U.S. Department of Energy (2013): most modern wind turbines have three blades. A 2-bladed rotor has to rotate faster for the same energy, which increases blade noise and loads, so the number of blades is a trade-off.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250215190637/https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1227612
- https://www.energy.gov/eere/wind/how-do-wind-turbines-work
  U.S. Department of Energy Wind Energy Technologies Office: horizontal-axis turbines most commonly have three blades, and the turbine pivots at the top of the tower so the blades face into the wind.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260324163001/https://www.energy.gov/eere/wind/how-do-wind-turbines-work
- https://wind-works.org/return-of-two-blades-a-new-look-at-an-old-idea/
  Paul Gipe, wind-energy author (2003): two-blade rotors present a dynamic or gyroscopic imbalance as the turbine yaws with changes in wind direction. Three blades effectively eliminate this imbalance, as does teetering the two-blade rotor at the hub.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260803153559/https://wind-works.org/return-of-two-blades-a-new-look-at-an-old-idea/
- https://granitegeek.concordmonitor.com/2021/06/25/why-do-wind-turbines-have-three-blades-why-not-just-two/
  Concord Monitor "Granite Geek" column (2021), quoting Earle Rich, who worked on early wind turbines: three blades give uniform thrust as the turbine yaws into the wind, while two blades make the tower periodically twist.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260613220842/https://granitegeek.concordmonitor.com/2021/06/25/why-do-wind-turbines-have-three-blades-why-not-just-two/
- https://www.tuev-nord.de/en/knowledge/explore/why-do-wind-turbines-have-only-three-blades/
  TUV NORD, a testing and certification company: a nine-bladed turbine would generate more electricity than one with three, but nowhere near three times as much. An odd number of blades avoids the imbalance strain, and two-blade turbines must run at high speeds and are noisy.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260516145424/https://www.tuev-nord.de/en/knowledge/explore/why-do-wind-turbines-have-only-three-blades/
