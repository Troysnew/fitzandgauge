# Sources: Why do some roads have little raised reflectors on the lines?

- https://www.txdot.gov/manuals/trf/pmh/material_selection/glass_beads-i1021584.html
  Texas Department of Transportation Pavement Marking Handbook: a glass bead returns light from a headlamp back to the driver. On wet roads the film of water scatters light before it can enter the bead, making wet markings nearly invisible at night.
- https://dot.nebraska.gov/safety/how-roads-work/pavement-markings-in-the-rain-where-did-they-go/
  Nebraska Department of Transportation: glass beads in striping paint reflect drivers' headlights back to them. When markings are covered by water during rain, they no longer reflect light back to drivers' eyes.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260516090722/https://dot.nebraska.gov/safety/how-roads-work/pavement-markings-in-the-rain-where-did-they-go/
- https://trid.trb.org/View/271981
  Transportation Research Circular 306 (1986), abstract only: raised pavement markers protrude above the surrounding pavement upward through the sheet of water.
- https://vtrc.virginia.gov/reports/all-reports/05-cr3/
  Virginia Transportation Research Council, Report 05-CR3 (2004): of six marking technologies tested, raised retroreflective markers outperformed the group under all conditions.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260710135324/https://vtrc.virginia.gov/reports/all-reports/05-cr3/
- https://vtrc.virginia.gov/reports/all-reports/96-r33/
  Virginia Transportation Research Council, Report 96-R33 (1996): snowplowable raised pavement markers are very visible in wet night conditions, and VDOT recommended continuing to use them.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260421223838/https://vtrc.virginia.gov/reports/all-reports/96-r33/
- https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/97152/ch01.cfm
  FHWA, Guidelines for the Use of Raised Pavement Markers (1998), chapter 1: cites a study noting the tactile and auditory warning provided by raised markers when drivers crossed the centerline, along with their retroreflective properties.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20251231152537/https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/97152/ch01.cfm
- https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/97152/ch02.cfm
  FHWA-RD-97-152, chapter 2: retroreflective raised markers use a corner-cube reflector lens and are rated by a coefficient of retroreflection. It cites Taylor et al. on an added rumble effect if the vehicle strays.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250217010432/https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/97152/ch02.cfm
