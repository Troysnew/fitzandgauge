# Sources: Why do fingers wrinkle in water?

- https://eprints.ncl.ac.uk/189064
  Kareklas, Nettle and Smulders, Newcastle University, Biology Letters (2013), peer-reviewed: participants moved submerged objects more quickly with wrinkled fingers than with unwrinkled ones, with no difference for dry objects. The authors see this as support for the idea that wrinkles may help with handling wet objects.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261020004731/https://eprints.ncl.ac.uk/189064
- https://www.bioedonline.org/news/nature-news-archive/science-gets-grip-wrinkly-fingers/
  Nature News (2013), republished by BioEd Online, Baylor College of Medicine: the common assumption is that water passes into the outer skin and makes it swell, but it has been known since the 1930s that fingers don't wrinkle when their nerves are damaged. Wrinkling is caused by blood vessels narrowing beneath the skin, and in the Newcastle test people handled wet marbles faster with wrinkled fingers.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260610031642/https://www.bioedonline.org/news/nature-news-archive/science-gets-grip-wrinkly-fingers/
- https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0084949
  Haseleu, Omerbasic, Frenzel, Gross and Lewin, Max Delbrück Center, PLOS ONE (2014), peer-reviewed: skin wrinkling did not improve dexterity in handling wet objects. The authors review evidence that wrinkling depends on the nervous system and suggest it may be a byproduct of blood vessels narrowing.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260901090918/https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0084949
- https://www.mdc-berlin.de/news/archive/2014/20140109-better_grip_with_wrinkly_fingers__mdc-rese
  Max Delbrück Center for Molecular Medicine press release (2014): wrinkling is an involuntary reaction controlled by the autonomic nervous system, in which tiny blood vessels in the skin constrict after water exposure. Testing 40 volunteers, the team found no grip advantage and could not reproduce the 2013 Newcastle result.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261020004830/https://www.mdc-berlin.de/news/archive/2014/20140109-better_grip_with_wrinkly_fingers__mdc-rese
- https://www.springermedizin.de/paradoxical-response-to-water-immersion-in-replanted-fingers/8780024
  Hsieh and colleagues, Clinical Autonomic Research (2006), peer-reviewed: in 20 replanted fingers without working nerve supply, the skin did not wrinkle in water, while the matching normal fingers did. The authors conclude the nerve supply is important in water-immersion wrinkling.
- https://www.the-scientist.com/the-science-behind-pruney-fingers-71410
  Danielle Gerhard, The Scientist (2023), quoting neurologist Einar Wilder-Smith: the water-soaking explanation is not the answer. After a long soak, blood vessels just below the skin constrict and pull the outer skin down into wrinkles, and fingers with nerve damage did not wrinkle.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260717222652/https://www.the-scientist.com/the-science-behind-pruney-fingers-71410
- https://www.discovermagazine.com/health/wrinkled-fingers-might-be-your-bodys-rain-treads
  Nathaniel Scharping, Discover (2017): when the nerves to the hand are cut, fingers stop wrinkling, and wrinkling happens as a result of constricting blood vessels. A 2011 paper proposed the "rain tread" grip idea, while a 2014 follow-up concluded wrinkles may simply be a by-product.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250427070110/https://www.discovermagazine.com/health/wrinkled-fingers-might-be-your-bodys-rain-treads
