# Sources: Why does a bridge freeze before the road?

- https://wxguys.ssec.wisc.edu/2017/11/20/bridges-ice-before-roads/
  The Weather Guys, Space Science and Engineering Center, University of Wisconsin-Madison (2017): a bridge hangs above the ground while a road rests on it. A bridge loses heat from its top, sides and bottom, while a road also gains energy from the ground, so the bridge cools faster.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20260420192252/https://wxguys.ssec.wisc.edu/2017/11/20/bridges-ice-before-roads/
- https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/INDOT/bulletins/1840aee
  Indiana Department of Transportation bulletin (2017): bridges are exposed to the elements on all sides, while a highway is insulated by the earth beneath it, so a bridge follows the air temperature closely and its surface falls below freezing very quickly. The original page is no longer online; the archived copy is linked.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20250205182611/https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/INDOT/bulletins/1840aee
- https://www.abc27.com/news/bridge-ices-before-road-but-why-we-asked-penndot/amp/
  ABC27 (WHTM, Harrisburg, Pennsylvania), quoting a PennDOT spokesperson: a bridge ices first because there is nothing under it and no ground attached to it, so whatever warmth the ground has doesn't reach it.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240516211850/https://www.abc27.com/news/bridge-ices-before-road-but-why-we-asked-penndot/amp/
- https://caps.ou.edu/reu/reu23/finalpapers/Guidry_FinalPaper.pdf
  Guidry, Tripp, Baldwin and Rosenow, National Weather Center research paper with OU CIWRO and NOAA's National Severe Storms Laboratory (2023): across 17 Ohio road-weather stations over one winter, roads were typically warmer than bridges near freezing, and when only one surface was below freezing it was most likely the bridge.
- https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/25905
  Nydahl, Pell, Lee and Sackos, University of Wyoming, for the Wyoming State Highway Department and FHWA (1984): heat pipes that carried heat up from the ground into a bridge deck raised its surface temperature and prevented the deck from freezing before the adjacent road.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20251007084703/https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/25905
- https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/part2c.htm
  FHWA, Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices (2009), Section 2C.32: the BRIDGE ICES BEFORE ROAD (W8-13) sign may be used ahead of bridges to advise users of winter weather conditions.
  Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20261019191505/https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/part2c.htm
