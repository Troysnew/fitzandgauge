Why do ceiling tiles have tiny holes?

Office ceiling tiles are full of tiny holes. Gauge wants to fill them in. What are they for?

▶ Premieres Fri Nov 27 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. USG, a manufacturer: water-felted mineral fiber ceiling panels have surface perforations that aid sound absorption. Fiberglass-core panels are also listed as strong absorbers.

    usg.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. CGC/USG Construction Handbook, chapter 9: the panel surface has perforations to aid in sound absorption. Absorbing sound and blocking it are rated separately, and high-NRC ceilings help make a space free from echoes and reverberations.

    usg.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Armstrong World Industries, a manufacturer: high-NRC ceilings can absorb sound within the space but cannot block sound from leaving or entering it. Absorption (NRC) and blocking (CAC) are separate ratings.

    armstrongceilings.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. US patent 3,137,364 (Wood Conversion Co., granted 1964): holes in acoustic boards collect sound waves and transmit them to the interior of the body, whose porous walls absorb sound.

    patents.google.com ↗

  5. The Construction Specifier, a trade magazine (2016): the surface must be porous, otherwise sound energy reflects off it and keeps propagating around the room. Trapped sound is converted to heat energy via friction in the internal pores or cells of a fiber structure.

    constructionspecifier.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. Building Enclosure, a trade magazine (2016): in porous absorbers sound energy is mainly converted into thermal energy by viscous friction, and perforated ceiling tiles are used with a porous absorber behind them.

    buildingenclosureonline.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗
