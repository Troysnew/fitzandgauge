Why are stop signs octagons?

Fitz wants a round stop sign. Gauge wants to know: is round already taken?

▶ Premieres Thu Nov 5 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. FHWA history: in the early 1920s, shapes were classified by danger; round for railroad crossings, octagon for STOP; drivers would respond to the shape even when they couldn't see the letters.

    webarchive.library.unt.edu ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. Texas A&M's Gene Hawkins: the shape plan was adopted in 1923; drivers could act on the shape without reading the words.

    magazine.northeast.aaa.com ↗

  3. FHWA: the octagonal shape is reserved for the STOP message.

    mutcd-dr.fhwa.dot.gov ↗

  4. The octagon is recognizable from the back.

    allstate.com ↗

  5. Second source: an octagon seen from behind is recognizable as a stop sign.

    99percentinvisible.org ↗
