Why do some power lines have big orange balls?

Some power lines carry big orange and white balls. Gauge thinks they're decorations. Who are they really for?

▶ Premieres Fri Dec 4 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Federal Aviation Administration, Advisory Circular 70/7460-1N, Obstruction Marking and Lighting (2026): marker balls make suspended wires more visible to pilots and should be used on transmission lines near airports and heliports and across rivers, canyons and lakes. Markers on those long crossings should be at least 36 inches across, and they alternate aviation orange, white and yellow, which gives the most visibility against all backgrounds. The circular is guidance, which must be followed where an FAA determination includes it.

    faa.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. FAA, Advisory Circular 70/7460-1M Change 1 (2024), the previous edition: gives the same 36-inch and 20-inch marker sizes, the same orange, white and yellow colors, and the same airport, river, canyon and lake locations.

    faa.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Rui Bo, associate professor of electrical and computer engineering at Missouri University of Science and Technology, in The Conversation, republished by Popular Science (2026): the balls help pilots see power lines so aircraft don't crash into them. Thin wires can be very hard to see from a plane or helicopter, especially when flying low, so the balls go where aircraft fly low, near rivers, valleys, airports and helicopter routes, and most neighborhood lines don't need them.

    popsci.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. ABC7 Los Angeles (2024): Southern California Edison crews installed colorful plastic marker balls to make power lines more visible to firefighting aircraft, at CalFire's request, near a helicopter water-fill point.

    abc7.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗
