Why are fire hydrants painted different colors?

Hydrant caps come in different colors. Gauge wants them all the same. Are the colors a code?

▶ Premieres Mon Nov 23 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. NFPA Public Input Report for the 2022 revision of NFPA 291, reproducing its text: hydrants should be classified by rated capacity (Class AA, A, B, C), with caps painted light blue, green, orange or red. Barrels are chrome yellow except where another color is already adopted, and the wording is "should".

    docinfofiles.nfpa.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. Mueller Co., a hydrant manufacturer: there is currently no federally mandated color coding of fire hydrants, and NFPA 291 recommends color-coding bonnets and caps by available flow. The NFPA standard is voluntary and many municipalities use their own systems.

    muellercompany.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Town of Georgina, Ontario, Fire and Rescue Services: marks bonnets and ports by flow at 20 psi residual (blue 1,500+ gpm, green 1,000-1,499, orange 500-999, red under 500), following NFPA 291. It is repainting public hydrants to meet it and only encourages owners of private hydrants.

    georgina.ca ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. City of Hastings, Nebraska, Utilities: an example of local variation, with a silver barrel and bonnet and caps coded by capacity according to the National Fire Protection Association recommendation, without a light blue band.

    cityofhastings.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. QRFS, a fire-protection supplier blog (weaker supporting source): NFPA 291 is a recommended practice and its color chart is not enforceable unless a jurisdiction decides it is. Some municipalities follow it strictly, some partly, some make their own code.

    blog.qrfs.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗
