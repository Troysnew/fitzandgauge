How do bees build hexagons?

Bees build their honeycomb out of hexagons. Gauge thinks circles would be better. Why six sides?

▶ Premieres Tue Nov 10 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Thomas C. Hales, "The Honeycomb Conjecture": among all ways to divide a flat plane into regions of equal area, the regular hexagonal grid has the least total perimeter. The proof also covers non-convex cells.

    arxiv.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. Gallo and Chittka (Queen Mary University of London): round cells such as bumblebees' waste space between cells, and honeycomb is optimal or very close to optimal for saving material. Describes two views on how the hexagon forms, and says whether bees use planning-like skills is unproven.

    frontiersin.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Gallo, Bridges, Woodgate and Chittka (2023): shared hexagonal walls give maximum storage with minimal wax, cells begin as circular and become hexagonal, and the building rules remain poorly understood.

    chittkalab.sbcs.qmul.ac.uk ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. Publisher page for the Gallo et al. (2023) paper above, in the Journal of Comparative Physiology A.

    link.springer.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. Karihaloo, Zhang and Wang (2013), read through its Europe PMC record: cells are circular at birth and become rounded hexagons, which the authors attribute to molten, visco-elastic wax flowing near the junctions, heated by worker bees.

    europepmc.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. Bauer and Bienefeld (2013): infrared and thermographic video showed bees directly constructing hexagons with antennae, mandibles and legs, and the wax stayed below the temperature the liquid-flow model needs.

    link.springer.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  7. Nazzi (2016), Scientific Reports: the hexagonal shape arises from the bees' construction behavior, and the paper proposes a behavioral rule, a third view on how the shape forms.

    nature.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  8. Phys.org coverage of the Cardiff University study, a cross-check: cells start as circles, and wax softened by worker bees flows and is pulled into hexagons at the three-wall junctions.

    phys.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  9. Discover magazine (2014), a summary of the Karihaloo paper that adds nothing new and serves only as a second cross-check.

    discovermagazine.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  10. WGBH (2013): circles leave gaps, only triangles, squares and hexagons tile a flat surface with equal sides, the hexagon is the most compact, and Hales proved the conjecture in 1999.

    wgbh.org ↗

  11. Science Friday educational resource: circles leave gaps, triangles, squares and hexagons share walls with no gaps, and Hales proved the conjecture in 1999.

    sciencefriday.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗
