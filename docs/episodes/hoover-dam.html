Case File #4 · Buildings · 13 min

Why is Hoover Dam full of pipes?

Case Files: more than 580 miles of pipe are sealed inside Hoover Dam. Solving why takes the river, the people who built it, and the water that fights back.

▶ Premieres Sun Nov 29 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Bureau of Reclamation, the dam's owner, Hoover Dam FAQ, "The Dam": "more than 582 miles" of one-inch steel pipe in the concrete, the hard, durable canyon rock, about 21,000 workers, the curve, water pressure of about 45,000 pounds per square foot at the bottom, "both gravity action and horizontal arch action", a weight of "more than 6,600,000 tons", the heat of setting concrete, "an estimated 215 blocks" forming a one-piece structure, and cooling finished in March 1935. Most of the dam's own numbers in this episode come from Reclamation, so they count as one voice, not several.

    usbr.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. National Historic Landmark nomination for Hoover Dam (National Park Service), prepared by a Reclamation archeologist in 1979 and revised by the Park Service in 1985: the pipe, the river's floods and the 1905 flood into the Salton basin, the dam's jobs of storing water and making power, its 660-foot base and its curve, the "ice-cold water" in the pipes, and the warning that the concrete would have taken "more than a century" to cool and "would have shrunk and cracked as it cooled". It contains some errors, such as "four tunnels on each side", which are not used.

    npgallery.nps.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗
