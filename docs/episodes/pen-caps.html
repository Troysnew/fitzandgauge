Why do pen caps have a hole in the top?

Gauge wants to seal that “pointless” hole for fresher ink. Fitz says not so fast.

▶ Watch it here Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. BIC: vented caps comply with ISO 11540, which aims to minimize the risk to children from accidental inhalation of pen caps.

    snopes.com ↗

  2. BIC's site: caps comply with international safety standards; ink drying is a popular assumption, not the main purpose.

    sciencealert.com ↗

  3. ISO 11540:2021 scope: reducing the risk of asphyxiation from caps of writing instruments likely to be used by children up to age 14.

    connect.snv.ch ↗

  4. BIC Cristal data sheet: ventilated cap reduces asphyxiation hazard.

    maketecheasier.com ↗

Transcript

Every word of the episode, exactly as it was fact-checked.

Gauge
This pen cap has a hole in the top. I'll seal it. Fresher ink!
Fitz
Wait. It's not really for the ink. BIC says it's for safety. If a kid breathes in a cap, a sealed one could block their airway.
Gauge
So how do caps stop that?
Fitz
A safety standard says caps on pens kids might use must let air through. So that hole is the fix.
Gauge
A tiny air tunnel!
Fitz
Yep. It's a backup, not a reason to chew caps.
