Why do jeans have little metal rivets?

Jeans have little metal dots at the pocket corners. Gauge thinks they're decoration. What are they for?

▶ Premieres Mon Dec 7 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. U.S. Patent No. 139,121, Jacob W. Davis and Levi Strauss & Company, May 20, 1873: pocket seams were usually ripped by the strain of hands going into the pockets. A metal rivet at each edge of the pocket opening binds the two layers of cloth together so the strain doesn't come on the thread.

    patents.google.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. U.S. National Archives, DocsTeach, patent drawing for Davis's "Fastening Pocket-Openings" (1873): tailor Jacob W. Davis adapted copper rivets from his earlier work on horse blankets to reinforce the weak points of work pants, and he and Levi Strauss patented the idea together.

    docsteach.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Levi Strauss & Co. archives, "Jacob Davis: His Life and Contributions": Davis, a Reno tailor, bought his cloth from Levi Strauss & Co. He used copper rivets to attach straps to horse blankets, decided to fasten pants pockets with the same rivets, and the joint patent was approved on May 20, 1873.

    levistrauss.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. Levi Strauss & Co., "The History of Denim" (2019): Jacob Davis came up with the idea of putting metal rivets at points of strain, like pocket corners, and the two men received patent 139,121 on May 20, 1873.

    levistrauss.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. Levi's, "A Riveting Story: Birth of the Blue Jean": men's work pants often split at the pockets and other stress points, Davis had the idea of rivets at those points, and he and Levi Strauss & Co. secured a U.S. patent on May 20, 1873.

    levi.com ↗
