Why is the bottom of a soda can curved inward?

Soda cans have a dome pushed into the bottom. Gauge wants to pop it flat. What is it for?

▶ Premieres Thu Dec 3 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. US Patent 4,294,373, Ball Corporation (granted 1981): the domed bottom gives stability against the internal pressure of the contents, since a thin drawn metal can tends to buckle outward under carbonated pressure. A flat bottom is not economically feasible, because the metal would need extra thickness to keep from deforming.

    patents.google.com ↗

  2. The Christian Science Monitor (2015), reporting the explanation of Bill Hammack, professor of chemical engineering at the University of Illinois: the bottom is pressed into a concave dome, which lets the can withstand greater pressures than if it were flat, and the internal pressure lets the can be strong in spite of its thin walls.

    csmonitor.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. US Patent 4,426,013, Jos. Schlitz Brewing Co. (granted 1984): under internal pressure, a can's bottom wall will stretch and bulge outward. Makers formed an upward dome because it resists deformation under internal pressure.

    patents.google.com ↗

  4. US Patent 7,398,894, Metal Container Corporation (granted 2008): the domed bottom has been found to resist deformation under internal pressure, and the bottom design must allow cans to be stacked in more than one layer.

    patents.google.com ↗

  5. US Patent 4,685,582, National Can Corporation (granted 1987): the bottom profile is designed so the bottom of one can nests within the end of the can below, which prevents wobbling when filled cans are stacked.

    patents.google.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. US Patent 5,477,977, Reynolds Metals Company (granted 1995): lists as a can-bottom design requirement the ability to nest against the seamed end of the can below when stacked, alongside standing stably on a flat surface.

    patents.google.com ↗

  7. Packaging School lesson (2025): to hold highly pressurized contents, an arc is formed into the bottom of the can, the dome, which spreads the pressure across that arc.

    packagingschool.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  8. Aluminium Guide: the bottom of the can takes a dome shape to resist the internal pressure, and the base is profiled so it stacks on the matching lid.

    aluminium-guide.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗
