Why do highway curves tilt?

Highway curves lean toward the inside. Gauge wants to flatten them. Why are they tilted, and why only a little?

▶ Premieres Wed Dec 2 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, section 6.3 (Rice University, 2016): on a level curve, friction provides the force that turns the car. On a banked curve at the ideal angle, the road's push has a sideways part that can turn the car without the aid of friction between the tires and the road.

    openstax.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. Texas Department of Transportation, Roadway Design Manual: a vehicle on a curve accelerates toward the center, and the road's banking (superelevation) and side friction between tires and pavement share the job of sustaining it. The maximum bank is normally 6 percent, or 8 percent where sharper curves are needed, with lower limits where very slow vehicles are common.

    txdot.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Washington State DOT, Design Manual chapter 1250 (2024): curves are usually banked to overcome part of the force acting on a vehicle. Where snow and ice build up regularly, banking is limited to 6 percent or less, because slow or stopped vehicles on steeper banks tend to slide inward, down the slope.

    wsdot.wa.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. Iowa Department of Transportation, Design Manual section 2B (2026): superelevation is the banking of a road along a curve so drivers can take it safely and comfortably. Iowa caps it at 8 percent, which reduces the risk of slow vehicles sliding down the bank in winter conditions.

    iowadot.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. HyperPhysics, Georgia State University: on a banked road, the sideways parts of the friction force and the road's push together provide the acceleration toward the center of the curve.

    hyperphysics.gsu.edu ↗
