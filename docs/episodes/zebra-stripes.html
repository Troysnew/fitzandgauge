Why do zebras have stripes?

Gauge is sure zebra stripes are camouflage against lions. The leading explanation is something much smaller.

▶ Premieres Tue Dec 1 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Caro and colleagues, "The function of zebra stripes," Nature Communications (2014), peer-reviewed; abstract on the University of Bristol research portal. Comparing five hypotheses across zebras, horses and their relatives, striping was associated with biting flies, with no consistent support for camouflage, predator avoidance, heat management or social interaction.

    research-information.bris.ac.uk ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. UC Davis News (2014), on the same study and quoting Tim Caro: the team ruled out camouflage, confusing predators, heat management and a social function, leaving biting flies. Caro says why flies avoid stripes is the next question.

    ucdavis.edu ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Science News (2014): outside expert Innes Cuthill of the University of Bristol called the fly idea the leading contender among the explanations, without calling the question closed.

    sciencenews.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. Caro and colleagues, PLOS ONE (2019), peer-reviewed: horses wearing striped coats got far fewer fly landings than when wearing black or white coats. Flies approached zebras without slowing down, and more of them touched rather than landed; the authors say the mechanism is unknown. The study used three zebras and a few horses on one farm in Britain.

    journals.plos.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. University of Bristol press release (2019), on the PLOS ONE study: horses in striped coats had fewer horse fly landings, and horse flies failed to slow down on approach to zebras. Tim Caro says the flies seem to fly over zebra stripes or bump into them.

    bristol.ac.uk ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. Melin, Kline, Hiramatsu and Caro, PLOS ONE (2016), peer-reviewed: zebra stripes are hard for large predators' eyes to resolve. In daylight, lions can make out the widest plains-zebra stripes only out to about 80 meters, and much less at twilight and night, so stripes appear highly unlikely to be camouflage against predators.

    journals.plos.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  7. Kojima and colleagues, PLOS ONE (2019), peer-reviewed, from the Aichi Agricultural Research Center and Kyoto University: cows painted with black-and-white stripes had significantly fewer biting flies on them than unpainted cows or cows painted only with black stripes.

    journals.plos.org ↗

  8. Mongabay (2019): the coat experiment ruled out smell and behaviour as the cause. Flies failed to slow down as they got close to the animals and bumped off, and how stripes work is still a hypothesis.

    news.mongabay.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗
