Why is there a little arrow by your fuel gauge?

There's a tiny arrow next to the fuel gauge. Gauge wants to scrape it off. What does it point to?

▶ Premieres Thu Nov 26 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Mazda CX-9 owner's manual: the carmaker states the arrow's direction indicates which side the fuel-filler flap is on.

    owners-manual.mazda.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. Ford 2007 Ranger owner guide, via ManualsLib: the arrow near the fuel pump icon indicates which side of the vehicle the fuel filler door is located.

    manualslib.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Jalopnik (Dec 2025): Ford's James Moylan had the idea in April 1986 after pulling a fleet car up to the wrong side of a pump in the rain. The arrow was to appear on 1989 model-year cars and did not become widespread until the later 1990s.

    jalopnik.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. Jalopnik (2018): Moylan, in April 1986, was soaked in the rain at the wrong side of the pump. The 1989 Escort and Tracer came first, with a possible earlier Mercedes W123 arrow.

    jalopnik.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. The Autopian (Dec 2025): Moylan's 1986 story, with the memo scan credited to the Henry Ford Museum. A Mercedes W123 low-fuel light was shaped as an arrowhead pointing to the filler side, but Mercedes had no records of it being intentional.

    theautopian.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. Jalopnik on why filler sides differ: Nissan's Steve Yeager says it is mainly a factor of fuel tank design, location and underbody packaging. Volkswagen puts passenger-car fillers on the right, away from traffic, and a Toyota engineer cites company policy for customer convenience.

    jalopnik.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  7. Motor1 (weaker source, cites Reddit, no named experts): some older cars do not have the arrow, and the pump-icon hose side is only a hint on some cars, not universal.

    motor1.com ↗
