How do woodpeckers not hurt their brains?

Woodpeckers bang their heads into trees thousands of times a day. How is their brain okay?

▶ Premieres Tue Nov 3 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Cell Press release for Van Wassenbergh et al., Current Biology, 2022: woodpecker heads work like stiff hammers and do not absorb the shock; the small brain keeps loads below concussion levels.

    sciencedaily.com ↗

  2. Cross-check of the same findings.

    phys.org ↗

  3. Beak and head stopped at the same rate in high-speed video; size is "the most compelling possibility".

    audubon.org ↗

  4. Woodpecker brains are about 700 times smaller than human brains; the hammer analogy; smaller animals withstand higher decelerations.

    smithsonianmag.com ↗
