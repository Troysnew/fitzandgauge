Why do some roads have grooves cut into them?

Some roads have grooves cut into them. Gauge wants to pave them smooth. Should he?

▶ Premieres Sun Nov 22 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Federal Highway Administration, Evaluation of Pavement Safety Performance (2015): grooving's primary objective is to improve drainage and mitigate hydroplaning. Several studies show grooved pavements reduce wet-weather crashes, with a caution that they did not control for regression to the mean.

    fhwa.dot.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. Texas Department of Transportation Hydraulic Design Manual: hydroplaning occurs when the drainage capacity of the tire tread and pavement surface is exceeded. Grooving may be a corrective measure for severe localized hydroplaning problems.

    txdot.gov ↗

  3. Louisiana DOTD glossary: grooving cuts slots into a pavement surface to provide channels for water to escape beneath tires, improving skid resistance and reducing the potential for hydroplaning.

    wwwapps.dotd.la.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. FHWA Technical Advisory T 5140.10 (1979, canceled 2005, so historical): both types of grooving effectively reduce hydroplaning potential, and longitudinal grooving is an effective means of reducing accidents at sites with high wet-weather accident rates. Wider, deeper grooves can be expected to increase the absolute noise level by a significant amount.

    fhwa.dot.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗
