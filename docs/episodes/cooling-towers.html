Why are cooling towers shaped like that?

Gauge thinks a cooling tower is pumping out smoke. What's that cloud, and why the curvy shape?

▶ Premieres Wed Dec 9 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission, Reactor Concepts Manual: tall hourglass-shaped natural convection cooling towers don't need fans, because hot air rises naturally as the circulating water splashes down inside. The "steam" from the top is really lukewarm water vapor, which forms a visible cloud when it meets cooler, drier air.

    nrc.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission glossary: a cooling tower is a heat exchanger that helps cool the water used to cool the exhaust steam from a power plant's turbines.

    nrc.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. U.S. Geological Survey, "Natural draft cooling tower": air is drawn through the tower by the density difference between the warm, moist air inside and the cooler air outside, which is why these towers tend to be tall. Modern units are called hyperbolic towers because of their shape.

    usgs.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. Wisniowski, Walentynski and Cornik, Silesian University of Technology, Bulletin of the Polish Academy of Sciences (2023), peer-reviewed: the chief advantage of the hyperboloid shape is its curvature, which gives the thin-walled structure good stability. A hyperboloid is a ruled surface, meaning it contains straight lines.

    journals.pan.pl ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. Practical Engineering, "Why Are Cooling Towers Shaped Like That?" (2024): the main reason for the shape is structural, since the double curve stiffens a thin shell against vertical loads and wind. Effects of the narrow waist on airflow are secondary, warm humid air rises without fans, and the plume is water vapor, not smoke.

    practical.engineering ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. J. B. Calvert, University of Denver (2007): the hyperboloid is a doubly ruled surface, so lattice towers of this shape can be built with straight columns. The plume often seen rising from the top is water, not smoke.

    mysite.du.edu ↗ · Archived copy ↗
