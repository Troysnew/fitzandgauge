Why are manhole covers round?

Gauge wants a square manhole cover to match the paving. Is round just tradition?

▶ Premieres Sat Nov 14 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Untapped Cities quoting the NYC Department of Environmental Protection: the principal reason covers are round is so they will not fall into the manhole. A non-round cover on a lip could drop in if inserted at an angle.

    untappedcities.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. KMPH, quoting Fresno Public Works Director Patrick Wiemiller: safety is the main reason, since a round cover cannot fall into the manhole but a square one could if turned diagonally. Removed round covers can be rolled instead of lifted or dragged.

    kmph.com ↗

  3. Science News (Ivars Peterson, 2003): the usual answer is that a circular lid will not fall through its opening, unlike a square or oval. A circle has the same width in every direction, and other constant-width shapes would also work.

    sciencenews.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. Mental Floss, quoting science teacher Larry Scheckel: a lip around the rim holds up the cover, a square, rectangular or oval cover could fall in if inserted diagonally, and round covers can be rolled and need no aligning.

    mentalfloss.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. JDP, a UK drainage supplier: round covers cannot fully fall through, can be rolled and do not need aligning. Square and rectangular covers exist and must be lined up with the edges.

    jdpipes.co.uk ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. Wikipedia, background only: in the UK nearly all covers are square or rectangular, and a lip makes the hole smaller than the cover.

    en.wikipedia.org ↗ · Archived copy ↗
