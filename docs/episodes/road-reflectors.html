Why do some roads have little raised reflectors on the lines?

Some road lines have little raised bumps on them. Gauge wants to scrape them off. What are they for?

▶ Premieres Sun Nov 29 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. Texas Department of Transportation Pavement Marking Handbook: a glass bead returns light from a headlamp back to the driver. On wet roads the film of water scatters light before it can enter the bead, making wet markings nearly invisible at night.

    txdot.gov ↗

  2. Nebraska Department of Transportation: glass beads in striping paint reflect drivers' headlights back to them. When markings are covered by water during rain, they no longer reflect light back to drivers' eyes.

    dot.nebraska.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Transportation Research Circular 306 (1986), abstract only: raised pavement markers protrude above the surrounding pavement upward through the sheet of water.

    trid.trb.org ↗

  4. Virginia Transportation Research Council, Report 05-CR3 (2004): of six marking technologies tested, raised retroreflective markers outperformed the group under all conditions.

    vtrc.virginia.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. Virginia Transportation Research Council, Report 96-R33 (1996): snowplowable raised pavement markers are very visible in wet night conditions, and VDOT recommended continuing to use them.

    vtrc.virginia.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. FHWA, Guidelines for the Use of Raised Pavement Markers (1998), chapter 1: cites a study noting the tactile and auditory warning provided by raised markers when drivers crossed the centerline, along with their retroreflective properties.

    fhwa.dot.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  7. FHWA-RD-97-152, chapter 2: retroreflective raised markers use a corner-cube reflector lens and are rated by a coefficient of retroreflection. It cites Taylor et al. on an added rumble effect if the vehicle strays.

    fhwa.dot.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗
