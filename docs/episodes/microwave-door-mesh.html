Why does a microwave door have a metal screen with tiny holes?

A microwave door has a metal screen full of tiny holes. Gauge wants clear glass instead. What does the screen do?

▶ Premieres Sat Dec 12 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. HyperPhysics, Department of Physics and Astronomy, Georgia State University (C. R. Nave): microwave ovens run at 2,450 MHz, a wavelength of about 12 cm. The holes in the door's metal screen are about 1 mm across, so the microwaves are effectively blocked, while visible light waves are far smaller than the holes and pass through.

    hyperphysics.gsu.edu ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign Department of Physics, Ask the Van (2008): most ovens use 2.45 GHz microwaves, a wavelength of about 12 centimeters. A thin metal screen with millimeter-size holes is an effective shield because the big waves can't get through the small holes, and microwaves pass easily through glass.

    van.physics.illinois.edu ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. Lou Bloomfield, How Everything Works (1996): the metal grid on the oven door reflects microwaves and keeps them inside, because microwaves can't pass through holes much smaller than their wavelength, while light has no trouble passing through.

    howeverythingworks.org ↗

  4. Lou Bloomfield, How Everything Works (2009): currents in the metal re-radiate the microwaves in the reflected direction, so the mesh acts like a metal surface, because the holes are much smaller than the 12.2-cm wavelength.

    howeverythingworks.org ↗

  5. U.S. Food and Drug Administration, Microwave Ovens (2023): microwaves are reflected by metal and pass through glass, paper, plastic and similar materials.

    fda.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗
