Why do bathroom sinks have a little hole near the top?

There's a little hole near the top of most bathroom sinks. Gauge wants to plug it. What's it for?

▶ Premieres Thu Nov 19 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. California Plumbing Code, sections 404.2 and 404.2.1, on a third-party code library site: the overflow pipe from a fixture connects to the house or inlet side of the fixture trap, so it joins the drain above the trap.

    gocodebook.com ↗

  2. Moen support article: overflow drains have a hole in the upper drain assembly that lines up with a channel in the sink. This prevents sinks from overflowing when the drain is closed or clogged.

    solutions.moen.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. B&Q (UK retailer) guide: the overflow is a small hole near the top of a basin that lets water drain away even if the plug is in. It is designed to stop a sink flooding the bathroom if the tap is left running.

    diy.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. Victorian Plumbing (UK retailer) guide: the overflow is a small hole, most commonly at the back of the basin under the tap, that drains excess water when the basin gets too full. A basin with an overflow needs a slotted waste.

    victorianplumbing.co.uk ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. Bob Vila (June 2026): the hole is a safety drain that prevents accidental flooding if the faucet is left running with the stopper closed.

    bobvila.com ↗
