Why do airport runways have big numbers?

Runways have giant numbers painted on each end. Gauge thinks it's a ranking. What do they mean?

▶ Premieres Thu Dec 10 Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. FAA, Aeronautical Information Manual, Chapter 2, Section 3 (Runway Markings): the runway number is the whole number nearest one-tenth of the runway centerline's magnetic azimuth, measured clockwise from magnetic north. Runway numbers are set from the approach direction.

    faa.gov ↗

  2. FAA Advisory Circular 150/5340-1M, Standards for Airport Markings (2019, Change 1 2020): the designator is the whole number nearest one-tenth of the magnetic azimuth seen from the direction of approach, so 183 degrees gives 18 and 87 degrees gives 9. A single-digit runway number is never preceded by a zero.

    faa.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  3. NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information (2017, updated 2021): runways are numbered by their magnetic compass reading rounded to the nearest 10 degrees, and the two ends point in opposite directions (18 for aircraft heading south, 36 heading north). The north magnetic pole drifts, which changes runways' magnetic bearings; Fairbanks renamed a runway in 2009 for this reason.

    ncei.noaa.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  4. NOAA NCEI, Magnetic Declination: magnetic declination is the angle between magnetic north and true north, and it changes over time and with location.

    ncei.noaa.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  5. NOAA NCEI, World Magnetic Model 2025 Released (2024): the Earth's magnetic field changes in unpredictable ways over time, so the navigation model is updated at least every five years.

    ncei.noaa.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  6. Jacksonville Business Journal (2011), reposted by the Jacksonville Aviation Authority: a shift in magnetic north meant new numbers for Jacksonville's runways (13-31 became 14-32, and 7-25 became 8-26). The drift requires periodic renumbering, and the old painted numbers were removed and new ones painted.

    flyjacksonville.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  7. Post Bulletin, Rochester, Minnesota (2023): Rochester's runway 2/20 was renamed 3/21 in May 2023 because magnetic north is a moving target, according to the airport's executive director.

    postbulletin.com ↗ · Archived copy ↗
