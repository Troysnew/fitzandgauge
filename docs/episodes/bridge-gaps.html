Why do bridges have bumpy metal gaps?

Those ka-thunk gaps are annoying, so Gauge wants to fill them in. What are they for?

▶ Watch it here Subscribe

Where this answer comes from

Each source below supports one claim in the episode. Links open the original pages.

  1. WVDOH State Bridge Engineer: bridges lengthen when warm and shorten when cool; a 100-foot bridge is about a quarter inch longer at 120°F than at 68°F; joints let the span move without damage.

    transportation.wv.gov ↗ · Archived copy ↗

  2. ADOT: joints let concrete expand and contract without cracking.

    azdot.gov ↗

  3. FDOT engineer: joints let the bridge move with temperature and limit damage.

    nbcmiami.com ↗

  4. AISC: cross-checks the expansion rate for a 100-ft span.

    mobile.modernsteel.com ↗

Transcript

Every word of the episode, exactly as it was fact-checked.

Gauge
These bumpy metal gaps on bridges? Annoying. I'm filling them in.
Fitz
Hold on. Bridges change size. Steel and concrete get longer when it's hot, shorter when it's cold.
Gauge
Bridges stretch?
Fitz
A bit. Engineers say a 100-foot bridge is about a quarter inch longer at 120 degrees than at 68. Fill the gap, and the bridge has nowhere to grow. Concrete can crack.
Gauge
So the gap is wiggle room!
Fitz
Yep. The bump is the fix.
